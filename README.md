
# Bike Purchase Analysis – Excel

This project analyzes customer demographic and behavioral data to identify patterns associated with bike purchases. The analysis was conducted using Microsoft Excel, with PivotTables, charts, and an interactive dashboard used to explore customer characteristics and purchasing behavior.

The project focuses on factors such as gender, marital status, income, number of children, age bracket, number of cars, occupation, education level, and commute distance.

## Dataset

The dataset contains customer demographic, financial, and behavioral information, along with whether each customer purchased a bike.

The analysis examines the relationship between bike purchases and the following factors:

* Gender
* Marital Status
* Income
* Number of Children
* Age Bracket
* Number of Cars
* Commute Distance
* Occupation
* Education Level
* Bike Purchase Status

The dataset was used to explore customer characteristics and identify patterns in bike purchasing behavior.

## Tools Used

* **Microsoft Excel**
* PivotTables
* PivotCharts
* Excel Dashboard

## Data Analysis

The analysis uses PivotTables and PivotCharts to explore bike purchase patterns across different customer characteristics.

The dashboard examines:

* Bike purchases by gender
* Average income by marital status and purchase status
* Bike purchases by number of children
* Bike purchases by age bracket
* Bike purchases by number of cars
* Bike purchases by commute distance
* Bike purchases by occupation
* Bike purchases by education level

## Dashboard

The Excel dashboard provides an interactive overview of customer bike purchasing behavior. It combines PivotTables and PivotCharts to make it easier to compare customer characteristics and identify purchasing patterns across different segments.

The dashboard includes visualizations for gender, marital status and income, number of children, age bracket, number of cars, commute distance, occupation, and education level. An interactive **Region filter** allows users to explore the dashboard for different regions.

## Key Insights

* **Income & Marital Status:** Among both Married and Single customers, bike buyers have a higher average income than non-buyers. Married buyers have an average income of approximately **$60,000**, compared with **$57,000** for non-buyers, while Single buyers average approximately **$55,000**, compared with **$51,000** for non-buyers.

* **Age Bracket:** The **Middle Age (32–53)** group represents the largest customer segment and has the highest number of bike purchases, with over **350 buyers**. Adolescents (0–31) and Older Adults (54+) show considerably lower purchase volumes.

* **Gender:** Males have slightly more bike purchases than females, with approximately **240 male buyers** compared with **235 female buyers**. Both genders show similar purchasing patterns, with non-buyers slightly outnumbering buyers.

* **Number of Cars:** Bike purchases are highest among customers who own **0 or 1 car**, with approximately **150 buyers** in each group. Purchase volume decreases considerably among customers who own 2 or more cars.

* **Number of Children:** Customers with **0 or 1 child** have the highest number of bike purchases, with approximately **140 and 95 buyers**, respectively. Purchase volume generally decreases as the number of children increases.

* **Commute Distance:** Customers with a **0–1 mile commute** have the highest number of bike purchases, at approximately **200 buyers**. For longer commute distances, non-buyers generally outnumber buyers, with particularly low purchase volumes among customers commuting 10+ miles.

* **Occupation:** **Professionals** have the highest number of bike purchases, with approximately **150 buyers**, followed by Skilled Manual workers. Manual workers show a higher number of non-buyers than buyers.

* **Education:** Customers with a **Bachelor's degree** have the highest number of bike purchases, with approximately **170 buyers**. Customers with Partial High School education have the lowest purchase volume.

## Recommendations

Based on the patterns identified in the analysis:

1. **Target Middle-Age Customers:** Focus marketing efforts on customers aged 32–53, who represent the largest purchasing segment.
2. **Target Higher-Income Customers:** Consider targeting higher-income customer segments, particularly Married and Single customers with higher average incomes.
3. **Focus on Short Commutes:** Promote bikes as a practical transportation option for customers with short commutes, particularly those traveling 0–1 mile.
4. **Target Customers with Fewer Children:** Consider tailored offers for customers with 0–2 children, who show higher purchase volumes.
5. **Focus on Low Car Ownership:** Position bikes as an alternative or complementary transportation option for households with 0–1 car.
6. **Target Key Customer Segments:** Professionals and customers with Bachelor's degrees represent strong purchasing segments and could be prioritized in targeted marketing campaigns.
7. **Explore Lower-Purchase Segments:** Further investigate why purchase volumes are lower among **Adolescents (0–31), Older Adults (54+), Manual workers, and customers with longer commute distances.**
